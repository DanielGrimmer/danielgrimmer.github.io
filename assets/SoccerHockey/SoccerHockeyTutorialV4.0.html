Basketball

A practice board. No duality — you are both looking at the same thing.

This teaches the interface, nothing more. Both of you can play it from this one screen, taking turns — use Swap sides if you would rather each drive your own player from the same laptop. The real game needs two devices and is where the interesting part lives.

Player 1 — up and right Player 2 — down and left
Player 1 to move.

Yellow squares are where you may move. A black O is a square the ball has already been — it can never go back there, which is what makes the game finite. The dots mark the squares that can reach a goal next turn: to score you must start your turn on one of them, and once they are all used up that goal is sealed. The board wraps around left to right, like Pac-Man.

Then the real game

Once the controls feel obvious, go and play the real one. You will need to be on separate devices, out of sight of each other's screen. That is not ceremony — it is the whole point.
